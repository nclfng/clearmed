---

## Why this project / what I hope you get out of it

- **You each own one agent, end to end.** By the end of the quarter you should be able to sit in an interview and explain how you designed it, what you tried, what failed, and how you validated it against real data. Create a story.
- **Real skills.** Depending on your agent you'll walk away with hands-on experience in: structured-output prompting, RAG / retrieval design, LLM-as-a-judge evaluation, human calibration and inter-rater agreement (Cohen's κ), controlled generation, and readability metrics. These show up in job postings.
- **How to run a small research project properly.** Freezing an eval set, measuring before you optimize, ablations, honest reporting. This transfers to basically any ML/DS work.

- building llm systems / genAI engineering is very trendy rn. a working, evaluated, multi-agent system puts you well ahead.

## What I'm asking for

- **Weekly updates.** A couple of slides with visuals, any prepared notes, and must always say what your next steps are. This keeps you on track, and it makes re-learning your own steps trivial when it's time to present. **Give me some kind of update every week, even a small one.** "I got stuck on X and here's where I am" and "here are my next steps" is a perfectly good update.
- **Take initiative.** I won't know everything and neither will you. Reach out with questions, and take the time to actually learn the topics and tools you're not familiar with.

## What "done" and "good" look like

- **Done** = runs from a clean checkout, result is logged, visuals included, and a teammate could follow what you did.
- **Good** = you can explain the choice you made and what you rejected; the numbers are on the *frozen* gold set
- A negative result, clearly measured and explained, counts as done and good.

## random other

- Highly recommend **HoloViews** for plots
- **Check the data.** Don't trust generated numbers, field names, or dataset claims without verifying against the actual source.
- Read the literature, get a feel for how researchers write. Skim a few papers in this space and look at how they frame the question, structure, present information, and limitations. Good starting points to search: **PLABA / plain-language biomedical adaptation** (Attal et al.), **MedAESQA** (the dataset paper), **LLM-as-a-judge** and its biases, **faithfulness / hallucination evaluation in summarization** (FaithBench), **health-literacy** studies from AHRQ / CDC.
